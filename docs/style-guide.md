# Style guide

How Money Rooms reads. The words are part of the product: they should make money feel lighter, never heavier.

---

## 1. Voice

- **Warm and plain.** Like a friend who happens to know finance. No jargon unless it's explained on first use.
- **Honest, not alarming.** Say what the numbers say. Never scold, never cheer falsely.
- **Short.** One idea per sentence. Most labels are three words or fewer.
- **Second person.** "Your take-home pay," not "the user's net income."
- **Never a verdict.** "Your plan is short by $3,200 a year starting at 82," not "Your plan fails."

## 2. Mechanics

- Sentence case for everything: titles, buttons, labels.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, parentheses, or hyphens.
- Buttons say exactly what happens: "Add account," "Save changes." The same action keeps the same name everywhere.
- Errors explain what happened and how to fix it. They never apologize and are never vague.
- Empty states invite an action: "No accounts yet. Add your first one."

## 3. Numbers

| Situation | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Dollars, whole | No cents | $4,120 |
| Dollars, large | Abbreviate above $1 million in summaries | $1.2M |
| Rates | One decimal at most | 6.5% |
| Ages | Whole years in summaries | Age 41 |
| Rough numbers | Prefixed with "about" | About $3,000 |
| Real vs nominal | Real by default; nominal labeled every time | $80,000 (today's dollars) |
| Cadence | Always shown | $4,120 / month |

## 4. Glossary

Use these words, exactly, everywhere. Never swap in a synonym on another screen.

| Use | Not | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Take-home pay | Net pay, net income | Pay after taxes and deductions |
| Gross pay | Salary (when meaning gross) | Pay before anything comes out |
| Spending | Expenses, outflows | Consumption only, not saving or debt payments |
| The gap | Surplus, margin | Take-home minus spending minus debt payments |
| Savings rate | | Saving as a share of take-home pay |
| FI date | Retirement date | The earliest you could stop working with the plan fully funded |
| Plan-to age | Life expectancy | How long the plan has to last |
| Today's dollars | Real dollars, inflation-adjusted | Amounts in current purchasing power |
| Future dollars | Nominal dollars | Amounts as they'll actually appear |
| Roughly | Estimated, approximate | A placeholder you can sharpen later |
| Look it up | | A number you can find, with where to find it |
| Not for me | N/A, skip | Doesn't apply to you, and counts as done |
| Best, likely, worst | Optimistic, base, pessimistic | The three projection bands |
| Account | Holding (when meaning account) | A place money sits or is owed |

## 5. Explaining a concept

When a term needs explaining, use this order: what it is in one sentence, why it matters to *you*, then where it comes from.

> **Plan-to age.** How long your plan needs to last. Running out at 88 is far worse than leaving some behind at 95, so this is set longer than average on purpose. You can change it.
